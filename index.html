The Tellum Charter version 1

The standard

Objectivity is the product's core requirement, not a tone. It means three specific things, and each is checkable. One: every figure is traced to a named source, or written as having none — it is never estimated. Two: where the record genuinely conflicts, the answer says so and states how lopsided it is, rather than staging a false balance. Three: every answer publishes what evidence would change it, so it can be proven wrong. We hold ourselves to this standard and publish the method so you can hold us to it too.

How answers are made

Each answer is held to one rule: state what the record shows, name where it is genuinely contested, and never assert a figure that is not in a source. A model retrieves and cross-checks the sources listed on the answer — the counts beside each answer say how many, and how many are primary documents — and drafts against them. It does not decide what is true: every claim is traced, or marked as having none. Confidence ratings state what they rest on. Flagging an answer re-runs the research through this method; it never edits the answer in place.

Who holds this charter

Today: the founders, openly. There is no community yet, and pretending otherwise would be a method violation. The charter is versioned, and no version is ever edited silently. The end state is community governance: amendments ratified only when contributors who historically disagree with each other both endorse them, so capturing the charter requires convincing your opponents, not outnumbering them. The published handover trigger: when a contributor base large enough to measure disagreement across has six months of rating history, charter amendments move to that ratification process. Missing that handover is a visible charter violation, not a quiet drift.

Where we currently fall short of it

Citation links resolve to the publishing organization rather than the specific document — the standard requires the document, and this is the largest open gap. The avoidance rating on each question is our editorial judgement, not a measurement against a coverage corpus, and is labelled as such. Naming these is part of the standard, not an exception to it.

This build

Headlines, briefs and answers are illustrative content written for this demo — not live reporting. Topics are drawn from genuinely contested 2025–26 debates; cited organizations and documents are real. Story images are generated marks, not photographs.
